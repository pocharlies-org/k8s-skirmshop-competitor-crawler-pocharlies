## Blocking Findings

1. `/api/competitors/due` returns top-level `leaseSeconds`, but the Synapse
   client drops it and the worker looks for row-level `claimLeaseSeconds`.
   Result: the proposed heartbeat never starts in production.
2. A duplicate `sync-start` is represented as `200 {ok:false, started:false}`;
   Synapse currently treats all successful HTTP responses as permission to crawl.
3. A heartbeat can refresh a claim after its lease expiry because its CAS does
   not enforce the cutoff. A stale worker can therefore revive ownership.
4. A rejected fenced result can still be followed by local cursor/recipe
   updates. The in-process fallback also bypasses start/heartbeat fencing.
5. The scheduler configuration advertises normal-sync `limit:24` and
   `concurrency:12`; concurrent normal/backfill ticks exceeded the required
   global cap even though the backfill workflow alone says `limit:4`.

## Required Remediation Gate

- Atomically enforce at most four non-expired competitor claims globally across
  normal and backfill dispatch; prove this with concurrent claim tests.
- Propagate lease data into the source event, reject all duplicate start forms,
  prevent expired-heartbeat resurrection, and stop all loser side effects after
  a stale result.
- Make the no-bus path fence tokenized rows equivalently to the durable path.
- Bound workflow input to four domains and two public fetches; deploy a separate
  GitOps PR reducing workers from six to four.
- Test every race above plus legacy tokenless compatibility and re-run the
  B2B manufacturer/distributor suite.
- Only after the RSO reviews merged/deployed evidence may F2 resume; F7 remains
  closed pending coverage convergence, two clean cycles per eligible domain,
  match quality, sustained Brain ingestion, and documented rollback.
